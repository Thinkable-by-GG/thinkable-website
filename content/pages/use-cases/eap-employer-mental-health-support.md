<p class="lead">Thinkable can help employers and EAP programs offer employees a private, structured way to begin working with mental health support before a situation becomes harder to manage.</p>
<h2>A private place to start</h2><p>Employees often wait until distress is high before they use a benefit or ask for help. Thinkable can create a lower-friction entry point with guided exercises and education that feel easier to begin.</p>
<ul><li>Self-guided support for stress, anxiety, OCD, and related concerns.</li><li>A private option for employees who are not ready for a direct conversation.</li><li>Resources that can complement EAP, therapy, coaching, and wellbeing programs.</li><li>Clear language benefits teams can use when introducing the resource.</li></ul>
<h2>Support beyond awareness campaigns</h2><p>Mental health campaigns can raise awareness, but employees also need something practical to do next. Thinkable can help turn interest into guided action.</p>
<ul><li>Provide structured tools after mental health awareness campaigns.</li><li>Give managers and benefits teams a simple place to route non-crisis support needs.</li><li>Support engagement between formal care or benefit interactions.</li><li>Help employees understand when additional care may be appropriate.</li></ul>
<h2>Partnership considerations</h2><p>Employer and EAP partnerships can explore privacy expectations, rollout messaging, employee access, and how Thinkable fits with existing benefits.</p>
<ul><li>Employee audience and support needs.</li><li>Launch and communication plan.</li><li>Relationship to current EAP or wellbeing vendors.</li><li>Reporting, privacy, and claims language that must be reviewed.</li></ul>
